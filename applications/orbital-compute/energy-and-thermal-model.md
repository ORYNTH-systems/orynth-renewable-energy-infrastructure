# Energy and Thermal Model Requirements

**Status:** PROPOSED — model inputs and equations are not yet populated with verified mission data

## Required separate budgets

The model must not collapse these quantities into one “GW” value:

1. **Array nameplate power (kW or GW):** rated output under specified conditions.
2. **Available electrical power (kW):** array output after orbit, pointing, temperature, degradation, and conversion effects.
3. **Stored energy (kWh or MWh):** usable stored energy, with charge/discharge limits and reserve.
4. **Compute electrical load (kW):** power delivered to processors, memory, and compute networking.
5. **Spacecraft support load (kW):** thermal pumps, avionics, communications, attitude control, and other required services.
6. **Rejected thermal power (kW thermal):** heat that radiator surfaces must actually emit under the modeled operating state.
7. **Delivered compute capacity (kW or GW):** compute load available at the stated boundary and duty cycle.
8. **Deployment rate (GW/year):** added capability launched during a period; never interchangeable with installed capacity.

## Minimum power balance

For every modeled orbit interval:

`available_array_power + storage_discharge >= compute_load + support_load + conversion_losses + storage_charge`

The model must expose each term and its uncertainty. It must include an eclipse case where relevant, peak and average loads, reserve policy, degraded-array conditions, battery aging, and fault conditions. If any required term is unknown, the result is OPEN rather than assumed to pass.

## Minimum thermal balance

The thermal model must identify heat generated by processors, memory, power conversion, pumps, and other onboard equipment; heat absorbed from sunlight, Earth infrared, and reflected sunlight; and heat rejected by each radiator configuration. It must state radiator view factors, orientation, emissivity, operating temperature, obstruction, and degraded performance.

The design must show that component temperatures remain inside declared operating limits through worst-case orbital and workload conditions. If heat rejection falls below the required rate, the workload must reduce, suspend, or shut down under an explicit control policy.

## Minimum communications and service balance

Compute capacity is useful only if the system can move the relevant data. Model sustained downlink and intersatellite-link capacity, pointing and availability, latency, ground-station coverage, routing loss, and safe behavior during link outage. Include radiation, component failure, servicing or replacement, collision avoidance, and end-of-life effects in the lifecycle boundary.

## Scale sequence

1. **Bench closure:** validate power distribution, storage behavior, thermal transport, radiator performance, and fail-safe control on a ground test article.
2. **Small orbital demonstration:** measure array output, eclipse behavior, thermal equilibrium, compute stability, radiation effects, and data links in orbit.
3. **Kilowatt-class replication:** demonstrate that multiple units preserve their individual and shared power, thermal, link, and control margins.
4. **Megawatt-class cluster:** close assembly, network, servicing, and fleet-failure models using measured unit data.
5. **Gigawatt-scale assessment:** publish a mass, launch cadence, orbital congestion, manufacturing, cost, replacement, and lifecycle case before adopting a gigawatt deployment requirement.

The stages are gates, not promised dates or proof that later scales will be achieved.
