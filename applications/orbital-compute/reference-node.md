# ORC-001: Orbital Solar Compute Reference Node

**Status:** CONCEPT — design basis for analysis; no flight qualification claimed  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Parent:** EMAH / AHME application profile

## Mission statement

Demonstrate useful compute in orbit using electrical energy generated by solar arrays mounted on the orbital system, with onboard storage for any eclipse or transient, and onboard thermal rejection. Ground systems may provide command, tracking, software, and data services, but do not supply operating electrical power to the orbital compute node.

## Reference point

SpaceX's filed June 2026 interview transcript describes a draft AI1 concept with 120 kW sustained compute, up to 150 kW peak, and stated first-order surface-density assumptions of 250 W/m² for arrays and 1,400 W/m² for radiators. These figures are **external comparison inputs**, not independently verified performance and not ORYNTH requirements. The first ORC-001 analytical model uses them to expose the closure questions; it does not assume they are sufficient.

Source: [SpaceX SEC-filed interview transcript](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026041761/fwp_cfointerview.htm)

## Candidate operating concept

- **Orbit:** compare a dawn–dusk sun-synchronous candidate with at least one alternative. Select only after propagating eclipse seasons, beta angle, Earth view, collision environment, coverage, and disposal constraints.
- **Power:** deployable photovoltaic wings feed a regulated DC bus. Power management allocates compute, thermal control, communications, attitude control, and avionics loads. Solar-charged storage covers eclipses and transients.
- **Compute:** modular payload with workload throttling and isolation. Treat the 120 kW sustained / 150 kW peak point as a sizing case for comparison, with a smaller subscale demonstrator first.
- **Thermal:** cold plates or equivalent interfaces move chip heat into a pumped fluid loop; the loop distributes heat to radiator surfaces with a clear view of cold space. Redundant pump paths and leak isolation are required in the design trade.
- **Communications:** optical intersatellite links and a separate route to ground. Compute may continue locally during a link interruption only if the workload's authority and data-retention policy permits it.
- **Control:** an independent effect gate checks current authorization, telemetry freshness, available electrical reserve, thermal margin, and link state before each consequential workload attempt.

## First-order sizing arithmetic

These calculations are deliberately simple screens, not final sizing:

1. If 250 W/m² is treated as effective array electrical output, 150 kW requires `150,000 W / 250 W/m² = 600 m²` at the stated condition. This does not include the additional array output needed for spacecraft loads, conversion losses, battery charging, degradation, pointing, or margin. The final array must be larger unless those loads are outside the stated power boundary.
2. If 1,400 W/m² is treated as effective radiator heat rejection, 120 kW of heat requires `120,000 W / 1,400 W/m² ≈ 86 m²`; 150 kW requires about 107 m². These are idealized area quotients only. Actual radiator sizing must include operating temperature, emissivity, view factor, Earth and solar loading, surface degradation, plumbing losses, peak duration, and design margin.
3. Compute electrical input is not automatically identical to radiator heat load. The thermal balance must account for power conversion and pump losses, absorbed environmental heat, and energy leaving the spacecraft in transmitted signals or other exported forms.

## Solar-only acceptance definition

The node qualifies as solar-only in operation only if:

- all electrical energy consumed during the declared mission interval originates from its admitted solar arrays;
- storage is charged only from admitted solar generation;
- no ground-supplied or beamed operating power is used;
- the energy balance closes over the full orbit and the declared degraded/end-of-life case;
- the system remains inside power, temperature, and storage limits, or enters its defined safe reduced-power state.

Command links and data links do not count as operating-power sources. A ground test configuration may use external laboratory power, but must be labeled as a test configuration and cannot be used as evidence that the flight configuration is solar-only.

## Component and interface blocks

| Block | Inputs | Outputs | Failure response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar array and deployment | Sun vector, attitude, array health | DC generation, telemetry | Isolate damaged string; recalculate power eligibility |
| Power conditioning and distribution | Array output, storage state, load requests | Protected bus feeds, metering | Shed lower-priority loads; inhibit new compute attempts |
| Energy storage | Solar charge, bus demand, temperature | Eclipse/transient support | Preserve survival loads; lower compute or shut down |
| Compute payload | Qualified power, valid workload authorization | Computation and attributable output | Throttle, checkpoint, isolate, or stop |
| Thermal transport and radiator | Payload heat, pump power, radiator geometry | Heat rejection, temperature telemetry | Reduce heat production; isolate loop; stop payload if limits approach |
| Attitude/orbit control | Orbit state, Sun/radiator pointing plan | Array and radiator orientation | Recompute energy/thermal margin; suspend workload if margins fail |
| Communications | Data queue, route and pointing state | Inter-node/ground transfer | Queue, reroute, or stop workload under data/authority policy |
| Effect gate and recorder | Signed request, live subsystem state | One-attempt release or block plus evidence | Fail closed on stale or contradictory state |

## Open design choices

- exact mission orbit and permitted eclipse envelope;
- compute module and workload profile;
- whether arrays and radiators can meet pointing needs on a shared bus or require independent articulation;
- cooling fluid, pump architecture, leak response, and radiator deployment geometry;
- usable battery chemistry, capacity, degradation, and eclipse reserve;
- radiation tolerance, shielding, memory integrity, and recovery approach;
- required sustained data throughput and service availability;
- end-of-life disposal, servicing, and replacement strategy.
