# External Target Register

**Status:** PROPOSED — source and scope records; not ORYNTH requirements

## SPX-TGT-DC-020GW — terrestrial power and cooling project target

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Attributed organization | SpaceX |
| Figure | 20 GW |
| Stated scope | Cumulative power-and-cooling level across a series of AI data-center projects |
| Stated horizon | By the end of the year following the 2026 Q2 earnings call (reported as end of 2027) |
| Qualification | Management called it tentative, said it did not expect the full 20 GW to be achieved, and estimated something closer to 15 GW at the power-plant level if some projects slipped |
| Classification | Terrestrial AI data-center infrastructure target; not an orbital solar generation target |
| Source status | Public earnings-call transcript; verify wording against the original recording before relying on it as primary evidence |
| Source | [SpaceX Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spcx/transcripts/692542-q2-2026/) |

**Mapping rule:** do not rename this figure “20 GW of orbital solar,” “20 GW orbital compute,” or “20 GW of renewable power.” The statement concerns power and cooling infrastructure for a series of projects. Its parts and measurement boundary require clarification before technical comparison.

## SPX-TGT-ODC-100GW-YR — orbital compute deployment ambition

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Attributed organization | SpaceX |
| Figure | Up to 100 GW per year |
| Stated scope | Long-term ambition to launch orbital AI compute capacity at an annual deployment rate; related prospectus language also describes deploying up to 100 GW of power to orbit annually |
| Design basis stated by company | Approximately 100 kW compute per metric ton; reusable launch capacity and larger deployable solar arrays |
| Qualification | Long-term company ambition. Filing says precise solar collection area, total satellite mass, and launch cadence remain under refinement; thousands of launches per year are contemplated at the stated scale under the filing's assumptions |
| Classification | Orbital deployment-rate ambition; not a 20 GW installed orbital fleet requirement |
| Source status | Company SEC prospectus; primary corporate filing, but company-stated forecast/ambition rather than independently validated performance |
| Source | [SpaceX SEC filing: orbital AI compute discussion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm) |

**Mapping rule:** preserve “per year.” Do not convert a deployment rate into installed fleet capacity, instantaneous electrical output, or annual energy without an explicit cohort, lifetime, utilization, and unit model.

## Relationship between records

The targets share organizational capabilities and some enabling systems: compute hardware, power equipment, cooling engineering, manufacturing, supply chains, and financing. Their physical systems remain distinct:

- the 20 GW statement concerns terrestrial data-center projects and their power/cooling buildout;
- the 100 GW/year ambition concerns orbital compute deployment and launch cadence;
- neither establishes that the other target is met;
- neither establishes a renewable-energy fraction or an orbital solar thermal design.

SpaceX's SEC filing says its terrestrial data-center operations rely significantly on natural gas and gas-turbine technology. Therefore, the 20 GW figure must not be tagged renewable without separate source evidence. See [SpaceX SEC filing on data-center energy supply](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm).

## ORYNTH treatment

These are external benchmarks only. They do not set ORYNTH's target, prove feasibility, or authorize a design decision. Any future ORYNTH requirement must be separately authored, unit-complete, scoped to a system boundary, and supported by admitted evidence.
